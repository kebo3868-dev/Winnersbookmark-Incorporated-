Field manual 4 tiers 15 modules 30 days

The ad's 15 lessons, rebuilt as a mastery curriculum.

Every module has the mechanism behind the skill, a drill run against your actual business — restaurant prospects, the audit offer, your content catalog — and a pass/fail mastery test. No module is complete until its test passes on live material.

Standing rule — read before Module 1

This only counts as expert training if the drills touch the market.

Learning Claude deeply is legitimate — it is the core competency the agency sells. But if this becomes "study the masterclass for a month," it is the production trap wearing a costume. Every drill below is anchored to a real prospect or a live piece of content. Week 4's graduation requirement is a closed audit, not a completed curriculum.

TIER I

Instruction

Getting a correct first draft on demand. Modules 01–04.
01

Context loading beats clever phrasing

Mechanism

Output quality tracks the raw material in the window, not the wording of the request. A vague ask over rich context outperforms a surgical ask over nothing, because the model is interpolating from what it can see. Most bad output is a missing-input problem misdiagnosed as a prompting problem.

Drill

Pick one live restaurant prospect. Assemble a context pack: their menu, their last 20 social posts, their Google reviews, their booking flow, their two nearest competitors. Paste all of it, then ask one lazy question — "what's costing them money?" Save the answer.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if the output names at least one problem you did not already know about, specific enough that the owner would recognise it. Fail if everything it says could apply to any restaurant. A generic answer means you loaded a generic pack.

02

Specify the artifact, not the topic

Mechanism

"Write about X" leaves the shape of the answer undefined, so the model picks the statistical average shape — an essay. Naming the artifact (length, format, reader, where it will be used, what happens next) collapses thousands of possible outputs into the one you can actually ship.

Drill

Take the audit offer. Write the same request three ways: "explain the audit," then "write the audit offer section of a cold email," then "write 90 words a busy restaurant owner reads on a phone between services, ending in one yes/no question, no adjectives about us." Compare.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if version three needs zero edits before sending. Fail if you rewrite any sentence — the edit you made is the constraint you forgot to state. Add it and re-run.

03

Assign the vantage point

Start here — this week
Mechanism

A role is not costume, it is a filter on what counts as relevant. "You are a restaurant operator who has been pitched by four agencies this year" changes which objections surface first. The useful roles are adversarial: the person who says no, not the person who helps you.

Drill

Feed your live pitch to a Claude instance framed as the specific owner you are currently trying to close — their margins, their staffing, their last bad vendor experience. Have it reject you three times with the real reason. Then rewrite the pitch to pre-empt objection one.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if one of the three rejections is an objection you have actually heard on a call. Fail if all three are polite abstractions like "not the right time" — that means you gave it a persona, not a person.

04

Critique-and-revise, never re-prompt

Mechanism

Re-prompting throws away the context you just built. Asking the model to grade its own output against a named standard, then revise, keeps the whole attempt in the window and turns one shot into a loop. The leverage is in the standard you name, not the number of turns.

Drill

Take your weakest performing piece of live content. One loop: "score this out of 10 on whether a restaurant owner would stop scrolling in the first two seconds, name the single biggest reason for the lost points, rewrite fixing only that." Repeat until the score stalls.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if you reached a version you would publish in under four loops. Fail if the score climbs while the writing gets worse — that is the model gaming your rubric, and it means the standard was vague.

TIER II

Thinking

Making the model reason instead of pattern-match. Modules 05–08.
05

Decompose before you delegate

Mechanism

A request that bundles research, judgement and writing gets all three done badly, because the model optimises the whole at once. Splitting the job into stages you can inspect means you catch the wrong turn at stage two instead of rewriting the finished thing.

Drill

Break your audit into its real stages — intake, diagnosis, prioritisation, costing, presentation. Run one prospect through stage by stage in separate turns, approving each before moving on. Note which stage you had to correct.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if you can name the one stage that consistently breaks. Fail if the answer is "all of it" — you have not decomposed, you have chunked.

06

Demand the mechanism before the answer

Mechanism

Conclusions are cheap to generate and impossible to audit. Requiring the causal chain first — why this would move that number — surfaces the assumption you disagree with while it is still editable, and gives you the sentence you will actually say on the call.

Drill

For each recommendation in your last delivered audit, force the chain: intervention → behaviour change → metric → revenue, with the weakest link flagged. Kill any recommendation whose chain has a step you cannot defend out loud.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if at least one recommendation dies. Fail if every chain holds — you either wrote a very good audit or you accepted the model's reasoning without pressure. Assume the second.

07

Constraints carry more signal than instructions

Mechanism

Telling the model what to do points at a broad region of output space. Telling it what is forbidden removes the defaults it would otherwise fall into — the agency clichés, the hedging, the tidy three-part list. Negative space is how you get voice.

Drill

Write your banned list from your own content catalog: every phrase, structure and opening move you never want to see again. Ten items minimum, pulled from real drafts. Attach it to your next three outputs.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if a stranger could tell two of your pieces came from the same shop. Fail if the banned list is generic writing advice rather than your specific tics.

08

Examples outrank descriptions

Mechanism

Describing a style requires you to correctly name what you like, which you usually cannot. Two or three real examples transmit rhythm, diction, length and structure at once, and let the model infer the rules you could not articulate.

Drill

Pull your three best-performing posts and your three worst. Label them. Ask for a fourth in the pattern of the good ones and explicitly not the bad ones, then ask it to state the rule it inferred.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if the inferred rule teaches you something about your own catalog. Fail if it just paraphrases your instruction back — the examples were too few or too similar.

TIER III

Systems

Turning one good output into a repeatable asset. Modules 09–12.
09

Prompts are inventory, not conversation

Mechanism

A prompt you rebuilt from memory is a prompt you are paying for twice. Saved, named and versioned, it becomes an asset with a quality floor that only ever rises — and the thing a junior can run without you.

Drill

Retire your ad-hoc prompting for one week. Everything runs from a file with five named prompts: prospect research, audit diagnosis, cold outreach, content repurpose, objection handling. Each with slots for the prospect's specifics.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if someone else can run the outreach prompt on a new prospect and produce something you would send. Fail if it only works when you are the one typing.

10

Real numbers in, judgement stays yours

Mechanism

Given no data the model will supply plausible data, and plausible data in a client deliverable is the fastest way to lose a room. Feed actual covers, actual ticket averages, actual review counts; let it do arithmetic and structure, and keep the call on what matters.

Drill

Build the opportunity model for one prospect using only figures you can source or that they gave you. Every number that is an estimate gets tagged as an estimate, in the deliverable, visibly.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if you can cite the source of every hard number under questioning. Fail if any figure exists because it sounded right.

11

Chain the work, hand off the artifact

Mechanism

Long single conversations drift and accumulate compromises. A chain — where each step's output is a clean, standalone document fed into the next step fresh — keeps quality flat across a long job and lets you restart any stage without redoing the others.

Drill

Run one prospect end to end as four separate sessions passing documents: research brief → diagnosis memo → audit deliverable → outreach sequence. No session sees the previous chat, only the document.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if each intermediate document reads as something you would show a client. Fail if any of them only makes sense to you — the handoff is where the process leaks.

12

Every deliverable ships through a harness

Mechanism

Taste is inconsistent under deadline; a checklist is not. A fixed set of pass/fail checks run against every outbound artifact catches the failure modes you already know about, which is most of them.

Drill

Write your six-check harness — unsourced numbers, banned phrases, generic claims, missing next action, wrong reading level, length. Run your last three deliverables through it and count the failures you shipped.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if the harness catches something in already-sent work. Fail if everything passes — your checks are softer than your standards.

TIER IV

Leverage

Where the skill becomes revenue. Modules 13–15.
13

Productise the workflow, not the tool

Mechanism

Clients do not buy access to a model, they buy a named outcome with a fixed price and a known turnaround. Any prompt chain that reliably produces something a restaurant would pay for can be wrapped as an offer — the chain is the cost structure, the outcome is the product.

Drill

Take your fastest chain and price it: name, deliverable, turnaround, price, what you refuse to include. Then time yourself running it once, for real, on a live prospect.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if your measured delivery time supports the price at a margin you would repeat fifty times. Fail if you cannot state the turnaround without hedging.

14

Your catalog is training data

Mechanism

Everything you have already published is a labelled dataset of what your market responds to, and most of it is unused. Mined properly it gives you voice, hooks and topic coverage without inventing anything — repurposing is not recycling, it is compounding.

Drill

Feed the full catalog in. Extract three things: the hooks that worked, the topics you have never covered, and the one argument you keep making badly. Produce this week's content only from that extraction.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if the week's output is published and at least one piece came from a gap you did not know you had. Fail if it stayed a document — an unpublished content plan is production dressed as strategy.

15

The whole stack, aimed at one deal

Mechanism

Skills held separately stay theoretical. Fourteen modules pointed at a single named prospect, in one week, with money on the outcome, is the only test that reports whether any of it works. This module has no separate content — it is the other fourteen under load.

Drill

One prospect. Context pack, adversarial pitch rehearsal, staged diagnosis, sourced opportunity model, harness-checked deliverable, outreach sequence, objections pre-empted. Send it. Book the call. Ask for the money.

Mastery test — pass / fail

Pass if an audit is sold. Nothing else passes. A brilliant deliverable that nobody bought is a fail, and the diagnosis is upstream — go back to whichever module the prospect's objection points at.

Weekly operating rhythm

Same shape every week, forever
Monday — Load

Build context packs for the week's three prospects. 90 minutes, capped. No writing, no pitching.

Tuesday — Diagnose

Staged audits through the chain. Every number sourced, every chain defended, harness run before anything leaves.

Wednesday — Send

Outreach goes out. Adversarial rehearsal first, one pass, then send. Nothing is redrafted after Wednesday noon.

Thursday — Talk

Calls only. Log every objection verbatim; those strings are next week's Module 03 input.

Friday — Publish

Catalog mining into published content. Ships Friday or does not ship. Then update the prompt library with what changed.

Weekend — Ledger

Two numbers only: audits sent, audits closed. Study time is not a number on this ledger.

The 30-day path

Modules are the syllabus; this is the order
Week 1

Module 03 first — it feeds the active closing sprint. Then 01, 02, 04. One prospect gets the full instruction stack.

Gate: 3 audits sent
Week 2

Modules 05–08 against last week's objections. The banned list and the example set get written this week and never rewritten from scratch again.

Gate: 1 recommendation killed
Week 3

Modules 09–12. The library, the sourced model, the chain, the harness. Volume should roughly double with no extra hours.

Gate: 6 audits sent
Week 4

Modules 13–15. Price the offer, mine the catalog, then point everything at one named prospect and close.

Gate: 1 audit closed
Graduation requirement

A closed audit. Not a completed curriculum.

If day 30 arrives with fifteen modules finished and nothing sold, the curriculum did not fail — it was used as cover. Restart at Week 1 with the same prospect list and half the reading.
